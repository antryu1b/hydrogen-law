Green hydrogen production

Electrolyzer for green hydrogen production in detail

Green hydrogen (H2) is a versatile energy carrier that can be applied to decarbonize a wide range of sectors. It can be used directly or in the form of its derivatives like e-Methanol, e-Ammonia, or e-Fuels to replace fossil fuels like coal or gas.

Only around 40% of global carbon dioxide (CO2) emissions originate from power generation which can be decarbonized via electrification. The other 60% of CO2 emissions originate from industry, mobility, buildings and others. These can be decarbonized via sector coupling, using green hydrogen and its derivatives to make renewable energy available to those sectors. This is why the production of sustainable hydrogen is so important for the energy transition.

Understanding the process of PEM water electrolysis

Our Proton Exchange Membrane (PEM), simply explained, splits water into its molecules hydrogen and oxygen using an electric current. The PEM membrane serves as barrier between the two electrodes and allows the protons to pass through while preventing the mixing of the gases, facilitating the separation and collection of the hydrogen and oxygen gases.

PEM water electrolysis is an important technology for the production of hydrogen as a clean and sustainable source, as it provides a way to generate hydrogen gas using renewable electricity and water, without producing greenhouse gas emissions.

Green hydrogen is clean hydrogen produced from water using renewable electricity, enabling zero-emission energy for diverse sectors.

It delivers low-carbon, flexible energy and helps decarbonize hard-to-abate industries, transport, and power.

Current drawbacks include high production costs, significant infrastructure needs, and some energy losses compared to direct electrification.

Green hydrogen is set to play a major role in the global drive for net-zero emissions and sustainable energy.

Green hydrogen is hydrogen created by splitting water into hydrogen and oxygen using renewable electricity, typically with Proton Exchange Membrane (PEM) electrolysis or other electrolysis technologies, resulting in virtually zero CO₂ emissions.

Green hydrogen enables decarbonization of hard-to-abate sectors like chemical or steel industry, refining, shipping, and aviation, and makes renewable energy available beyond the power grid via sector coupling.

PEM (Proton Exchange Membrane) electrolyzers use a membrane to separate hydrogen and oxygen gases while allowing protons to pass, harnessing volatile renewable energy with high efficiency, flexibility, and power density.

Siemens Energy has decided to expand its electrolyzer portfolio. The new Elyzer P-360 will complement, rather than replace, the Elyzer P-300. Both products offer distinct advantages that can be leveraged depending on project-specific requirements.

The Elyzer P-300 will remain the preferred choice for projects with comparatively high electricity costs, as its efficiency is already strong and is expected to improve further. The Elyzer P-360, on the other hand, will be the optimal solution for CAPEX-sensitive markets. With a total installed cost (TIC) advantage of approximately 20%, the Elyzer P-360 is expected to be competitive not only in applications with moderate pressure requirements, but also in use cases characterized by medium annual full-load operating hours.
